The auction · Toyosu 豊洲 · verified 2 August 2026

Tsukiji tuna auction? It moved. Here's how to actually see it.

The auction left Tsukiji in October 2018 and runs every trading morning at Toyosu, 2.5 km east. The internet still sells the old address. This page is the current mechanics: the free walkway that most people should use, the 100-a-day lottery deck, the 5 a.m. transport puzzle, and what auction tours can and cannot legally sell you.

TL;DR

  • The free 2F walkway is the plan, not the fallback. Tokyo's official FAQ says there is no limit on walkway numbers — open from 05:00, no booking, glass-side view of the whole floor.
  • The lottery deck is a bonus: 100 people a day, groups of ≤5, apply ~a month ahead, photo ID for everyone. Lose the draw and you've lost nothing — the walkway still works.
  • Transport is the real gatekeeper. The Shimbashi Yurikamome starts too late (05:45). Toyosu station's 05:15 first train or the 05:02 trading-day bus are the moves — details in chapter 06.

Three myths this page exists to kill

“Watch the tuna auction at Tsukiji fish market”

Moved in 2018. The auction runs at Toyosu Market. Tsukiji's outer market stayed — go there for food, not the auction.

“There is no lottery in December”

Wrong — and it's a tour listing's own small print saying it. The December lottery runs (apply early November). The real rule: no applications for 25 Dec–5 Jan, which is why the famous 5 January first auction is walkway-only.

“Viewing is 3 groups of 40, ten minutes each”

Superseded years ago. The current official application PDF says 100 people per day in groups of up to five, window 05:45–06:25. Sites quoting 120 are reciting 2018 press releases.

Option 2 · the bonus

The lottery observation deck

Floor level, auction-side, the fish an arm's length away. Free — but you apply about a month out and win one of 100 daily places. Mechanics in chapter 05.

Two smaller truths worth knowing: the assembly point for deck winners is the PR Corner — used at 05:30 even though its published hours start at 08:30 (a trap if you check the hours and panic), and two cafés inside the market open at 04:00 (Iwata and Senriken) — the sanctioned way to kill the pre-auction hour. The sushi breakfast comes after, not before.

The auction morning, minute by minute

  1. 04:00Iwata and Senriken open inside the market — coffee and toast at wholesale-worker prices.
  2. 05:002F observation walkway opens; buyers inspect tail-cut tuna on the chilled floor below.
  3. 05:15First train leaves Toyosu station; Shijō-mae 05:18. Deck winners head to the PR Corner assembly.
  4. 05:30Bells ring; auctioneers start the rapid-fire chants. Deck window runs 05:45–06:25.
  5. 06:30Auction winds down; turret carts stream the tuna to the intermediate wholesalers.
  6. 07:00Restaurant queues form — Sushi Dai's storefront slot system has been running since 05:00.
  7. 09:00Done by nine — which is exactly when Tsukiji's outer market hits its stride, 2.5 km west. Do both in one morning.

The lottery, mechanically

The application portal runs on a fixed monthly rhythm. From the official FY2026 schedule (published only as an image — we transcribed it):

  • 100 winners per day, groups of up to five — and a baby counts as one of the five. Representative must be junior-high age or older.
  • Photo ID for every person, matched against the application. The winning email cannot be resent — screenshot it.
  • No same-day entry, no waitlist, no second round. Lose the draw → take the walkway, lose nothing that matters.
  • Dress warm: the deck sits at the chilled auction floor's temperature — near freezing, in any season.
  • The lottery runs on every one of the market's opening days (August 2026: 20 recruitment dates = exactly the 20 trading days). Check the calendar before applying.

Agencies can't apply for you. The official FAQ answers this question directly: the programme presumes direct application by participants, and the terms prohibit reselling or transferring viewing rights. That's the legal floor under everything in chapter 07.

Auction tours, honestly

Since no operator can sell deck access, what does a paid "tuna auction tour" actually sell? Three legitimate things: a guide on the free walkway who can read the hand signals and hierarchy for you, the solved 5 a.m. logistics, and breakfast with a queue strategy. That's real value at dawn in an unfamiliar wholesale district — just know it's what you're buying. The full comparison lives on the tuna auction tour page; the short version:

  • Toyosu auction morning + Sushi Dai or Bentomi breakfast (★ 4.6 · 151 reviews · ~$115, August 2026 figures) — the exact dream-morning format: walkway viewing, then the storefront-slot sushi counters handled for you. Best for first-timers who want the whole dawn choreographed.
  • Private Toyosu + Tsukiji morning with auction viewing (★ 4.8 · 292 reviews · ~$167) — both markets in one sweep with a private guide; the right shape if you want the Tsukiji food crawl attached to the auction dawn.
  • DIY at ¥190: this page + the calendar + the 05:15 train replicate most of it free — that's not a sales pitch failing, it's the honest baseline every tour above has to beat.

FAQ

No — it moved to Toyosu in October 2018. Tsukiji's outer market stayed, and it's still the better breakfast; they pair perfectly in one morning.

Online, roughly a month out: the window opens at 15:00 around the first Wednesday of the preceding month and runs about a week; results the following Thursday at 18:00. 100/day, groups of ≤5, photo ID everyone, screenshot the winning email.

No — the rules bar agency applications and resale of viewing rights. Tours sell the walkway + logistics + breakfast, which is legitimate; guaranteed-deck claims are not. Full honest breakdown: tuna auction tours.

On site ~05:15–05:30; the action peaks 05:30–06:30. Transport is the constraint — see getting there for the 05:02 bus and 05:15 train that make it possible.

Only from the free walkway — the deck lottery takes no applications for 25 Dec–5 Jan. The 2026 hatsuseri set the all-time record: ¥510.3 million for a 243 kg Ôma bluefin.
