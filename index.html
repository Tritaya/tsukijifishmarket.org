TL;DR — the three things that fix most Tsukiji plans

  • Two markets, one morning. The tuna auction is at Toyosu (5:30 a.m., free walkway); the eating is at Tsukiji (hits stride at 9). They're 15 minutes apart and pair perfectly.
  • Check the date, not the rule of thumb. Five calendars govern these markets, "closed Sundays" has exceptions, and the official page shows the wrong month for dates too far out — our calendar page has the exact 2026 dates.
  • Go early, carry cash, store the suitcase. Pros have priority until 9, fresh stalls shutter from 2 p.m., and the Plat Tsukiji lockers close at 4 sharp.

The myth wall — things guides still print

Every claim below is stored verbatim from circulating guides and corrected from an official source. It's the fastest way to check whether whatever you read elsewhere is current.

“Tsukiji Fish Market closed in 2018”

The wholesale half moved. The outer market's 400-odd shops never closed — what actually happened.

“Watch the tuna auction at Tsukiji”

At Toyosu since 2018 — free walkway, no booking. The mechanics.

“Sushi Dai is in the outer market”

It's a name collision: the famous counter moved to Toyosu; a separate Sushi Dai Honkan trades at Tsukiji. Both explained.

“Closed every Sunday and holiday”

Special trading days exist (three in 2026, including a Sunday), and Sunday runs a real reduced lineup. Exact dates.

“The market shuts down Dec 30–Jan 4”

Backwards: 25–30 Dec is the outer market's peak week — the wholesale market closes for New Year. Year-end truth.

“Tsukiji banned tourists and tours”

A December-2025, operator-targeted request — not reissued since. The chairman's actual ask was a time-share. The real story.

“It's cash-only”

Cash-first, not cash-only — we measured all 333 listed shops; some are even cashless-only. The numbers.

“Aritsugu has been at Tsukiji since 1560”

That lineage is Kyoto's. Tsukiji Aritsugu dates to 1918 — still venerable, differently. The knife houses.

Plan the morning

  1. 05:15First train to Shijō-mae for the auction walkway — or sleep in and start at step three with zero regret.
  2. 07:00Auction seen; queue ticket taken at the famous counters, or breakfast at the 4 a.m. cafés.
  3. 09:00Cross to Tsukiji as pro-priority ends — 15 minutes by train. The food crawl begins.
  4. 11:00Crush builds. Shift to the knife and dried-goods lanes, or the shrine's memorial mounds.
  5. 13:00Late lunch as day-trippers peak — then Hama-rikyu gardens or Ginza, ten minutes away.
  6. 14:00Fresh stalls shutter. If the day went sideways (rain, closures): Senkyaku Banrai never closes.

Booking a December trip? Read visiting responsibly first — the year-end crush has its own etiquette, and in 2025 the market formally asked tour operators to pause that month.

When a tour genuinely beats DIY

Both markets are free and this site is the manual — so we'll say it plainly: most visitors don't need a tour. Three cases where paying wins:

  • The auction dawn. Transit barely works at 5 a.m. and the bidding is unreadable without help — exactly what the auction-morning products solve (from ~$115, breakfast queues included).
  • Eating with context. A tastings-included walk (~$80–100) buys stall relationships and stories; the $13 guide-fee-only walks buy orientation at pocket money.
  • Making, not just eating. Sushi classes (from ~$50) are the rainy-day and kids answer — and the format that doesn't crowd the lanes.
