Tsukiji 築地 vs Toyosu 豊洲 · checked 29 October 2026

Tsukiji or Toyosu? Choose by the hour you can face.

The tuna auction is at Toyosu, and it starts at 5:30 in the morning. The street breakfast is at Tsukiji, where the market association asks visitors to come from 9:00. If you want to watch tuna being sold, set the alarm for Toyosu. If you want to eat your way down a lane of stalls, sleep in and go to Tsukiji. If you can manage both, do Toyosu first: the two fit into one morning, and the route between them is below.

Which should you choose?

The two markets stopped being one place on 11 October 2018, when Tokyo's wholesale fish trade moved from Tsukiji to the new market at Toyosu. What stayed behind at Tsukiji is the outer market: a few blocks of shops, stalls and small restaurants that grew up around the old wholesale site and kept trading after it left. So the question is not which market is better. It is which half of the old story you came for.

  • Choose Toyosu if the auction is the point. The bidding for large fish, tuna included, starts at 5:30. You watch it through glass from a second-floor visitor gallery that opens at 5:00, costs nothing and needs no booking. Full detail on the tuna auction page and the building guide to Toyosu Market.
  • Choose Tsukiji if breakfast is the point. No alarm and nothing to book. The market association asks visitors to leave the early hours to professional buyers and recommends 9:00 to 14:00. What to order is on the food page.
  • Do both if you have one free morning. Toyosu at dawn, then Tsukiji from about 9:00. The order is forced by the clock: the auction is over before Tsukiji's visitor hours begin. The plan is in chapter 03.

One honest caution about Toyosu: it is a working facility, not an attraction that happens to sell fish. Visitors stay on the corridors and look down on the trading floors, and the experience is the view, not the bustle. Expect the old Tsukiji crush and Toyosu will feel cold. Go knowing you are watching a wholesale market at work, and the view of the tuna floor is the reason to be there.

Both in one morning: the clock

This is the plan most first-time visitors should follow if they can face the start. Every time below comes from a timetable or an official notice, not from a tour brochure.

  1. 05:00The second-floor visitor gallery over the auction floor opens on every market day. No lottery and no limit on numbers.
  2. 05:15First Yurikamome train from Toyosu Station, at Shijo-mae at 05:18. The first train from Shimbashi leaves at 05:45, which is too late for the start of the bidding, so stay near Toyosu or start from there. Other ways in, including the early bus and a taxi, are on getting there.
  3. 05:30Bidding for large fish, tuna included, begins in Block 7, the Fish Wholesale Market Building. Lottery winners meet at the same time at the PR Corner on the 3rd floor of the Block 7 Management Facilities Building.
  4. 05:45The observation-deck window for lottery winners runs to 06:25. Everyone else keeps watching from the gallery.
  5. 06:30Breakfast inside Toyosu if you want a seat: the restaurants are on the 3rd floor of Block 6 and of the Block 7 management building, and the market's shops are on Block 6's 4th floor. Queues for the famous counters work by their own rules: see sushi breakfast.
  6. 08:15Cross to Tsukiji. Toei bus route Ichi-01 toward Shimbashi leaves from the stops inside the market; on the weekday timetable we read for 29 October 2026 it ran several times an hour between 7:00 and 9:00. Get off at Kokuritsu Gan Kenkyu Center-mae, the stop the outer market's own access page names.
  7. 09:00Tsukiji's visitor window opens. Eat at the stall where you bought the food or inside the eatery, keep large bags in a locker, and shop for knives and tableware while the lanes are still manageable.

If you would rather sleep until a normal hour, the trade-off is simple. Skip the auction and give Tsukiji the morning. Toyosu without the auction is mostly corridors, restaurants and shops: worth it on a rainy day, less so otherwise.

The lottery, if you want the floor-level deck. It is free and runs month by month. For visits in December 2026, applications open at 15:00 on 4 November and close at 23:59 on 10 November, and results come out at about 18:00 on 19 November. Groups are up to five people (a baby counts as one), 100 people a day are drawn, and every person shows photo ID on the day. No travel agency or tour can apply for you, there is no waitlist, and no applications are taken for visits from 25 December to 5 January. Losing the draw costs you nothing: the gallery is still open. Where the two markets are

Tsukiji sits on the west bank of the Sumida River, beside the old wholesale site. Toyosu is across the water to the south-east, on the Yurikamome line. In a straight line they are under 3 km apart.

Which to book, if you want a guide

Neither market needs a guide to get in. What a guide adds is different at each one: at Tsukiji it is knowing which stalls to try and handling the order; at Toyosu it is the 5 a.m. logistics and someone who can read the bidding for you.
