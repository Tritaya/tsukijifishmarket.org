Hours & calendars 休市日 · verified 2 August 2026

Tsukiji opening hours? There's no such thing. There are five calendars.

Five different organisations publish five different calendars for five adjacent places, and picking the wrong one is how visitors end up photographing shutters on a Wednesday. This page tells you which calendar governs which building, the exact 2026 closed dates, the day's closing ladder, Sunday's shifted clock — and the traps built into the official pages themselves.

TL;DR

  • The best default: 09:00–11:00 on a trading Tuesday, Thursday or Friday. Before 09:00 you're inside professional buyers' hours; after 14:00 the fresh-food market is functionally over.
  • Never trust a weekday rule. The market closes Sundays, public holidays and some Wednesdays — but in August 2026, Wednesday the 12th trades while the 5th, 19th and 26th are shut. Only the published date grid resolves it: the exact 2026 dates are in chapter 04.
  • Each building has its own calendar. The Uogashi building shuts every Sunday and holiday even when the lanes outside trade; Toyosu runs its own 114-closed-day calendar; Senkyaku Banrai never closes at all — it's the rainy-day and closed-day answer.
  • “Closed” is not the whole story. The calendar has a third state: temporary opening days, when the market deliberately trades on a Sunday or national holiday. Three exist in 2026 — chapter 03.

The five calendars, and who owns each one

Ask “is Tsukiji open?” and you're really asking five questions about five places run by five organisations. Here is each one's rule, and how to check your date against the authority that actually publishes it.

1 · Outer-market lanes 場外市場

NO DISTRICT-WIDE RULE · SHOP BY SHOP

The NPO running the district states plainly that its calendar is just the wholesale market's calendar, and that each shop's own schedule is the real authority. Most shops close Sundays, holidays and the designated Wednesdays; a handful trade 365 days.

Check your date: the market association's calendar at tsukiji.or.jp (red cells = closing days), then the individual shop's page in its official store list.

What's worth eating in the lanes

2 · Tsukiji Uogashi building 築地魚河岸

CLOSED SUN + HOLIDAYS + SOME WED · BLDG 05:00–15:00

A Chuo City facility with its own published rule: building open 05:00–15:00, all shops trading 07:00–14:00, and — the trap — closed on every Sunday and public holiday even when the lanes outside are busy. Tourists are asked to come after 09:00; no eating on the 1st floor.

Check your date: the building's own page at tsukiji.or.jp/uogashi.

The Uogashi building, floor by floor

3 · Toyosu seafood market 豊洲市場

OWN TMG CALENDAR · 114 CLOSED / 251 OPEN IN 2026

The Tokyo Metropolitan Government publishes its calendar annually — including a machine-readable CSV with one row per closed date, importable straight into a phone calendar. Visitor access 05:00–17:00 on open days, but restaurants mostly close by ~14:00.

Check your date: the metropolitan government's calendar page at shijou.metro.tokyo.lg.jp (seafood CSV, or the English one-page PDF).

Visiting Toyosu Market

4 · Toyosu produce market 青果

118 CLOSED IN 2026 · 4 EXTRA WEDNESDAYS

Not the same calendar as the fish floor: in 2026 the produce half closes on four extra Wednesdays — 14 October, 4 November, 25 November and 30 December — when the seafood market trades. Its free visitors' deck has a real 06:30 auction with almost no tourists.

Check your date: the produce CSV on the same metropolitan government calendar page.

Where the produce deck fits in a Toyosu morning

5 · Senkyaku Banrai 千客万来

NO CLOSING DAYS · RETAIL 10:00–18:00

The outlier that rescues a badly-timed trip: its own FAQ states there are no closing days at all — it trades even on Toyosu Market's closed days, pausing only for equipment inspection or weather. Restaurants to 22:00, free foot-bath garden 09:00–22:00, the attached onsen 24 hours.

Check your date: no check needed — that's the point.

Senkyaku Banrai in full

There's even a sixth, nested calendar: the Plat Tsukiji information centre runs its own hours and posts its own ad-hoc closures — six in August 2026 alone (5, 14, 15, 16, 19 and 26 August), on a Japanese-only notice page, while its English listing still says “open everyday”. If you're storing luggage or a stroller there, treat that “open everyday” line as unreliable — details on the practical page.

The three-state discovery: closed, open — and specially opened

Every English guide treats the calendar as binary. It isn't. Reading the official calendar's day classes directly (verified 2 August 2026), a third state exists: the temporary opening day 臨時開市日 — a Sunday or national holiday on which the market deliberately breaks its own rule to relieve a holiday cluster. Three are flagged for 2026:

  • Wednesday 6 May 2026 — a substitute public holiday at the end of Golden Week, opened to break the three consecutive closed days of 3–5 May.
  • Wednesday 23 September 2026 — the Autumnal Equinox, a national holiday, opened to break Silver Week's three-day gap (20–22 September closed). The market reopens on a national holiday.
  • Sunday 27 December 2026 — opened inside the year-end buying peak, producing seven consecutive trading days from 24 to 30 December.

This kills the flat rule “always closed Sundays and holidays” — and it cuts the other way too: a national holiday that falls on a would-be trading day usually closes the market. The date grid is the only truth.

“The market is closed on Wednesdays”

Some Wednesdays. Two to four a month, irregularly: August 2026 closes the 5th, 19th and 26th but trades on the 12th; November closes only two; December closes four and then trades on Wednesday 30 December.

“Sundays and public holidays are always closed”

Temporary opening days exist. 6 May, 23 September and Sunday 27 December 2026 are all flagged “Temporary opening day” on the official calendar — two of them holidays, one a Sunday.

“Toyosu is closed 116 days a year”

114 in 2026 for the seafood market (251 open), counted from the official CSV; the produce market has 118. The round 116 circulating in guides has no 2026 referent.

“Tsukiji closes for New Year from about 30 December”

Backwards. 25–30 December is the outer market's all-shops-open peak week. The wholesale market closes 31 December; the outer market's 31st is a discretionary trading day. Full story in chapter 07.

The daily rhythm: at Tsukiji you plan around closings, not openings

The district doesn't publish an opening time; it publishes a declared operating model — three bands that explain the entire day. The crowd cannot legitimately arrive before 09:00, so it arrives at 09:00; the shops close from around 14:00, so everything compresses into five hours:

BandOfficial labelWhat it means for you
06:00–09:00Professional buyers' hours 業務用仕入れメインYou may walk the lanes, but the first of the official eight manners asks you to keep out of buyers' way. Quiet, atmospheric — and someone else's shift.
09:00–14:00Public buyers' hours 通常販売時間The visitor day. All five hours of it.
14:00–Flexible trading 自由営業時間Fresh food gone; snack stalls, discounting and a shrinking cast of survivors.

The ladder below is the practical version, every time from an official shop listing or the association's own pages:

  1. 04:00The earliest shutters rise — shops open between roughly 04:00 and 06:00, and several famous tamagoyaki names fire their griddles 04:00–05:00. Kaisen Donya Tsukiji Kanno, the earliest opener on the official list, opens 04:30 and runs to 21:00.
  2. 06:00Professional buyers' band begins. You may walk the lanes, but the official manners code asks you to stay out of buyers' way — you're a guest in someone's supply chain. Kitsuneya's horumon-don counter opens around 06:30 and is gone by 13:30.
  3. 08:00The snack tier wakes: several of the best stalls open only now, and Maguroya Kurogin — the district's headline tuna counter — starts at 08:00 (or 08:30).
  4. 09:00The public band opens and the crowd is released. 09:00–11:00 is the sweet spot: full stock, moving lanes, shopkeepers with time to talk. (08:00–09:00 is quieter still, if you tread lightly.)
  5. 11:00Peak. Shoulder-to-shoulder until about 13:00; queues at famous counters stretch to 30–60 minutes.
  6. 12:30The sell-out closers: Kurogin's own listing reads “ends as soon as it runs out” at 12:30, and popular items are sometimes gone before noon. Dry goods die even earlier — several katsuobushi, pickle and rice shops shut 11:30–12:30, and one rice shop's entire day is 09:00–11:00.
  7. 14:00The wall. Fresh-food shops shutter en masse; the Uogashi building's all-shops-trading window ends. The market is functionally over — for fresh food.
  8. 15:30The survivors' hour ends: snack and grill stalls run to about 15:30 with discounting (half-price fatty-tuna selections were observed on a June 2026 visit — a dated observation, not a standing offer). Yamacho's tamagoyaki griddle trades 06:00–15:30 with no closing days.
  9. 20:00+The long tail: Uni Tora to 20:00, Kanno to 21:00, a few tourist-facing restaurants reopen for dinner — and Sushizanmai Honten never closes at all: 24 hours, 365 days.

The pattern to internalise: nothing here has one opening time, but almost everything has a hard closing — and the closings cascade from 12:30. Arrive at 09:00 and the whole ladder is above you; arrive at 13:00 and most of it has already happened. What to actually eat on that ladder is the food page's job; the famous counters' queue maths lives on the sushi breakfast page.

So when should you arrive?

  • The foodie who wants the market, not the meal: 07:00–08:00. Kitsuneya and the early counters open, the professional traffic real and visible, essentially no queue anywhere. Keep to the edges of the lane — and accept that several of the best snack stalls don't open until 08:00, so a dawn arrival buys atmosphere at the price of choice.
  • The shopper who intends to buy and ask questions: 09:00–10:30. The band the official pages point at, and the hours when a shopkeeper can afford to explain a product to you.
  • The dry-goods or knife buyer: 09:00–11:00 and no later. That trade closes earlier than the food trade — several shops shut between 11:30 and 12:30 — and buying a knife properly takes real staff time.
  • The sushi-breakfast visitor: 07:00–08:30 — counters open, emptier, cheaper. Routes on the sushi breakfast page.
  • The casual visitor who won't get up: 13:30–15:00. Genuinely pleasant, genuinely cheap, genuinely thin. You forfeit the fresh counters entirely.

Sunday runs on a shifted clock, not an empty one

The closed-day paradox, which almost no English guide states: on Sundays, holidays and the designated Wednesdays, the shops that do open absorb the entire undiminished crowd — so a closed day produces longer queues and a smaller menu at the same time. “It's closed, so it'll be quiet” is exactly backwards.

Still, about a third of shops open — mainly eateries — and “a third of shops” is not folklore; it's checkable. Shops declaring no closing days at all on the official store list (verified 2 August 2026 — self-declared hours, known to drift):

  • Sushizanmai Honten & annex — 24 hours, year-round; the district's standing fallback
  • Tsukiji Sushi Ichiban and Goban (ramen) — both 24 hours
  • Kaisen Donya Tsukiji Kanno — 04:30–21:00, the earliest opener in the district
  • Uni Tora — 07:00–20:00 · Uogashi Senryo — 08:00–15:30 · Uogashi-don Kanno — 08:00–15:00
  • Yamacho — 06:00–15:30, which is why it absorbs the Sunday tamagoyaki crowd
  • Tsukiji Iroha — 06:30–14:00 · Tsukiji Gyu-take — 06:30–15:00

And the clock itself shifts. From the same official records: the unagi diner opens 09:00 on Sundays instead of 07:30; MATCHA STAND MARUNI moves from 08:00 to 09:00; Marutake runs a short Sunday day of 08:00–14:00. Others close earlier — Mata-koi-ya at 15:00 instead of 22:00, Hokkai Banya loses its evening service entirely. A few actually run longer at weekends. Practical translation: a Sunday visitor at 07:30 finds a nearly empty district; the same visitor at 09:30 finds a smaller, later, busier market. Sunday's real usable window is 09:00–14:00 — an hour later at both ends than a weekday. Remember the Uogashi building is shut every Sunday regardless, and so is Toyosu; Senkyaku Banrai is the one door that's always open.

The year-end truth: peak week, not shutdown

The single most-repeated calendar error about Tsukiji is that it closes for New Year “from about 30 December”. The association's own year-end plan says the opposite: 25–30 December is all-shops-open peak trading, with shops opening from around 06:00 and the council itself suggesting you take the first train. In 2026 the calendar goes further — Sunday 27 December is a designated temporary opening day, so the run from 24 to 30 December is seven consecutive trading days, the longest unbroken stretch of the year, deliberately engineered for the New Year ingredient rush.

The council's published phasing (2025 edition — the latest issued):

  • To ~12 December: relaxed; weekdays especially recommended.
  • 15–25 December: building; come as early in the day as you can.
  • 25–30 December: the peak — all shops open from ~06:00. If you're shopping, this is the week; if you're sightseeing, it isn't.
  • 31 December: a “free trading day” — some shops closed, some on short hours. The wholesale market is closed on 31 December 2026; in 2026 its New Year closure ran 1–4 January with the hatsuseri first auction on the 5th, and the 2027 calendar was not yet published as of early August 2026.

The same guidance carries two unusually concrete safety notes worth repeating verbatim in spirit: watch for pickpockets — put your wallet deep in your bag after paying, not somewhere quickly reachable, because pickpockets watch the transaction — and agree a meeting point with children and older relatives in advance, because “every year, without fail, someone gets lost”.

Two quieter year-end facts. First, the pressure valve: in the same December 2025 news cycle in which Tsukiji asked visitors to defer, Senkyaku Banrai was reported actively welcoming year-end visitors — and it has no closing days, so the whole 1–4 January gap is its best argument. Second, money: bank branches and their ATMs follow the New Year shutdown too, so 1–4 January is the one window when cash-only planning genuinely bites — convenience-store ATMs stay available.

The December advisory, in past tense on purpose: in December 2025 the association asked tour guides to refrain from guiding, food-walking tours and group shepherding for that month — the first such request in its history — while its vice-chairman stressed on television that ordinary shoppers remained welcome. That request was December-2025-scoped and had not been reissued as of early August 2026; whether a 2026 edition appears should be re-checked from late November.

The association's own December workaround for shoppers: come in the early afternoon, because the dawn queues form for the first-thing-in-the-morning goods. Context on the visiting responsibly page.

Calendar traps — including the ones on official pages

The 90-second date check, consolidated. Before booking anything for a specific day:

  1. Open the market association's calendar at tsukiji.or.jp with your month in the ?date= parameter and read the red cells — and confirm the month heading matches what you asked for (see trap 1 below).
  2. Going to Toyosu? Download the seafood CSV from the metropolitan government's calendar page — one row per closed date, and it imports straight into a phone calendar, which turns the whole problem into a notification.
  3. If one specific shop is the point of the trip, open its page on the official store list and read its own Closed and Hours rows — shops are the final authority, not the district.
  4. Nothing here can be reserved, ticketed or queued for in advance — there is no booking step to protect you from a wrong date. The calendar check is the booking.
  • The official calendar silently lies about future months. The tsukiji.or.jp calendar accepts any ?date= value, but for months beyond the published year it renders the current month with no error or warning. Verified 2 August 2026: requests for January, April and May 2027 all returned an August 2026 grid. Anyone planning Golden Week 2027 from that page will read August's closures and never know. Always read the month heading.
  • 2027 is simply unpublished. No 2027 calendar existed anywhere as of early August 2026 — the metropolitan government's 2027 page included. The 2026 dates appeared around September 2025, so a similar timing is plausible for 2027's, but that is an expectation, not a confirmed date.
  • The annual PDF calendars are images. Closed days are conveyed by colour only; they cannot be machine-read. The machine-readable truth is the metropolitan government's CSV.
  • Official sources contradict each other. Tokyo's tourism board says Toyosu admits the public until 3 p.m.; the market authority itself publishes 5 p.m. (believe the operator — but remember 17:00 is building access, not lunch). The Uogashi food hall is 07:00–19:00 on one official page and 07:00–15:00 on another; treat 15:00 as the real planning deadline. And most English guides print one set of hours for “Tsukiji”, silently merging the lanes with the Uogashi building — which is how visitors meet a locked building on a trading Sunday, and why flat “closed Wednesdays” claims overstate what the date grid actually says.
  • Shop-list hours are self-declared and drift. The official store list is authoritative for closing-day rules and weak on exact times — one record contradicts itself within a single listing. When a specific shop is the whole point of your trip, check its own page the week you fly.

If you'd rather not manage any of this: the cheapest guided option in the pool — the Tsukiji guided walking tour (★ 4.8 · 1,828 reviews · ~$13, food bought as you go) — only runs on trading days by construction, which quietly solves the calendar problem for the price of a bowl of ramen. It suits the first-timer with one morning who'd rather spend it eating than reconciling five calendars; everyone else can do this page's homework free. More formats on the market walks listing.
