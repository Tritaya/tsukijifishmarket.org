TL;DR

  • The auction itself is free. The 2F walkway at Toyosu is unbooked and officially uncapped — a tour adds narration, not access.
  • The deck cannot be bought. Free monthly lottery, self-application only, resale banned. Any listing implying otherwise is wrong — full mechanics on the tuna auction page.
  • Pay for the dawn, not the door. The 5 a.m. transport puzzle and the breakfast queues are where a guide genuinely earns $115–150 — and where DIY costs you planning instead.

The rule that sorts honest listings from the rest

Tokyo's official FAQ answers the exact question — can travel companies apply for the lottery? — with a flat no: the programme presumes direct application by participants. The application terms go further, prohibiting transfer, resale, and "recruiting companions for profit to sell viewing rights", with refused entry as the sanction. That's the whole game: deck access is structurally unsellable.

“Tour includes tuna auction viewing access”

Means the free walkway. Which you have anyway. The honest listings say so plainly; the rest let you assume.

“There is no lottery in December”

A real product's own terms say this — and it's false. December's lottery runs (apply 4–10 Nov 2026); the true rule is a 25 Dec–5 Jan blackout on visit dates. Trust the official schedule over listing small print.

“We'll register the lottery for you”

Against the rules — applications are personal, ID-checked at the gate against the application. A guide may coach your application; nobody can file it for you.

What a good auction tour is actually for

  1. LogisticsThe 5 a.m. problem is real: Shimbashi's Yurikamome starts too late, the 05:02 bus skips ordinary Sundays, and taxis need pre-booking sense. A tour deletes the whole puzzle — that alone is most of the fee. (DIY version: getting there.)
  2. NarrationFrom the walkway, the auction is fast, silent theatre — hand signs, bell codes, a caste system of buyers. A guide who can read it turns twenty confusing minutes into the best thing you did in Tokyo.
  3. BreakfastThe famous counters run same-day storefront queues from 05:00 (the slot system explained) — tours that "include Sushi Dai or Bentomi" are selling you that queue handled, which on a one-shot morning is worth real money.
  4. The bridgeThe best formats then cross to Tsukiji as it opens — auction dawn at Toyosu, eating morning at Tsukiji: the complete story in five hours.
