Visiting responsibly · Tsukiji 築地 · verified 2 August 2026

“Tsukiji banned tours”? No. Here's what the market actually asked.

In December 2025 the outer market's own trade council did something no Tokyo food destination had done before: it publicly asked guided tours to stand down for the month. It was a request, not a ban; it was aimed at operators, not at you; and it has been switched off since. This page tells that story straight, prints the eight official manners verbatim, and explains the handful of rules that genuinely surprise people — because knowing exactly how narrow the ask was is the difference between visiting respectfully and repeating a stale headline.

TL;DR

  • Nothing is banned and nothing is in force. The December 2025 advisory was a first-ever, December-scoped request to tour operators — and as of 31 October 2026 the banner carrying it is literally commented out of the council's own homepage HTML, with no 2026 reissue. Never read it in the present tense.
  • The whole year-round compliance surface is eight published manners — ten minutes' reading, official English, printed in full below. Keep those and you are the visitor the eight manners describe — and, as it happens, the one who gets its regulars' prices rather than its visitor prices (is Tsukiji a tourist trap?).
  • Two rules are stricter than most guides tell you: the official map says most retailers ban photography outright (assume no, ask anyway), and walking-while-eating is stated as a flat prohibition on the council's own press page — eat at the shopfront, not on the move. Details in chapter 04.
  • Groups: the rule is “small numbers”, no official cap. 3–4 in single file is fine; 8+ moving as a block is the failure mode. For 20–50 the council sells its own ¥500-a-head classroom — chapter 06.

The December 2025 story, told straight

On 26 November 2025 the NPO that runs the outer market — the Tsukiji Food Town Development Council, together with the shopping-street association — published a notice on its site (tsukiji.or.jp) addressed to tour guides and operators. It asked them to refrain, through December, from three named activities: guiding, eating-stroll guiding, and group leading. The strongest line, in the English rendering that circulated via SoraNews24: “Please refrain from conducting guided tours (tours for sightseeing, eating strolls, group guidance)… If this is not abided by, we may be forced to contact the police.” The Japanese original ends 「守って頂けない場合には、やむを得ず、警察等の緊急出動を要請します。」 — a promise to treat a blocked lane as a public-safety incident, not a penalty against any visitor.

Why then, why at all? The council's chairman, Yoshitsugu Kitada, put it on the record: infrastructure was never installed to accommodate tourists, and since the Tokyo Olympics word of mouth had brought so many visitors that he called it a state of overtourism. The trigger was concrete: at year-end 2024 the crowds reached packed-in density, injuries occurred, pedestrians spilled into the carriageway so that a guard had to shout a path open for each passing vehicle — and long-standing trade regulars started shopping elsewhere. The December squeeze itself is decades old (TV archives show police directing trucks through the same year-end crush in 1986); what changed is its composition, and the crowd-accident risk that comes with it.

Three details the headlines dropped. First, the council's vice-chairman, Masahiro Terade, drew the line himself on TV Asahi: “we are absolutely not saying ‘don't come’” — December is simply very busy, and the meaning was restraint for safety's sake. Second, Kitada gave the actionable version almost no English guide carries: his ideal December is a time-share — shoppers from 09:00 to around noon, tourists at leisure after that. That is a schedule you can actually follow. Third, the market did not speak with one voice: one seafood-dining shop refused year-end guide bookings from the outset, while another shopkeeper said openly she wanted both shoppers and tourists to keep coming. In the same news cycle, Toyosu's Senkyaku Banrai — different management entirely — publicly welcomed year-end tourists.

How to say it correctly: “In December 2025 the market association asked tour operators to stand down for the month — the first request of its kind. It expired with December, the banner is switched off, and it had not been reissued for 2026 as of 31 October 2026.” Anything in the present tense — “Tsukiji bans tours”, “tourists told to stay away” — is quoting a switched-off notice as a live rule. The eight manners 築地のマナー8箇条 — official English, verbatim

Published year-round by the council and reprinted in the footer of every page of its site. The preamble sets the register: Tsukiji is a wholesale district where professionals do serious business with professionals. This is the entire year-round rulebook — keep these eight and there is nothing else to comply with.

01

Professional buyers take priority in the morning!

“Priority should be given to professional buyers until 9:00 a.m. If you see people going around the market and purchasing goods, try not to get in their way.” The 09:00 boundary drives the whole daily rhythm.
02

Stay inside the eatery or in front of the store when eating!

“Wandering around while eating can bother passerby. Please eat inside the eatery or in a space designated for eating.” Buy, stop, finish, then move.
03

Leave your luggage behind!

“Large bags can obstruct the flow of people—please store them in a coin-operated locker… Strollers can be stored at the Plat Tsukiji information center for temporary safekeeping (500 yen per stroller).”
04

Move in small numbers!

“The Tsukiji Outer Market has narrow streets and small shops. Please refrain from moving around in large groups. Hold your child's hand when walking.” The one manner the December advisory escalated.
05

Ask before taking pictures!

“Ask the shopkeeper for permission before taking photos of a shop. Some shops have signs that prohibit photography.” The map is stricter still — see chapter 04.
06

Don't touch the foods!

“Touching them or poking them with your fingers will lower their commercial value… especially raw foods. If you need to check something, ask the shopkeeper.” Open trays are stock, not samples.
07

Don't try to bargain!

“Carefully-selected products are sold at wholesale prices. Bargaining over the price is not customary at Tsukiji.” On a hand-forged knife an opening offer reads as an insult.
08

Smoke only in designated areas!

“Smoking is permitted only in designated areas. Do not smoke while walking around or litter cigarette butts.” Backed by Chuo City's street-smoking ban; the market's one smoking area is inside Plat Tsukiji.

Two of the eight are backed by more than courtesy: street smoking is banned by city ordinance, and the no-eating-while-walking rule appears elsewhere on the council's own site as an outright prohibition. And there is a separate, standing three-point protocol for tour guides on the same official page — no guiding inside a shop without prior permission or blocking an entrance; free samples assume genuine purchase intent; guiding without watching your surroundings causes accidents — plus a request that first-time guides consult the Plat Tsukiji information centre before running a tour.

The rules that surprise people

“Photography is fine as long as you ask politely”

Expect a no. The website's manner #5 says ask first — but the council's official English map (edition 2026.2) says flatly: “Most of the retailers have NO PHOTO and NO MOVIE policy.” Both are official and current; the map is the stricter and newer artefact. So: assume no by default, ask anyway, accept a refusal without negotiating — and know that buying something small first turns most refusals into a yes. No-photo signs are usually Japanese-only.

“It's a street-food market — eating while walking is the point”

It's a published prohibition. The council's press page states walking-while-eating is prohibited (食べながら歩く事を禁止しております), and the map says “stay at the shop until you finish your food.” Nobody will stop you; the rule really asks you to stop moving. Sanctioned places to eat what you bought: the map's marked Eating Areas, the Plat Tsukiji rest benches, the Tsukiji Uogashi rooftop plaza and its 3F food court, and the Tsukiji Side Street terrace at 4-10-17.

“Free samples are there for tasting”

Samples assume purchase intent. Official wording: “Stores provide food samples free of charge under the assumption that customers are actually considering purchasing the products.” That sentence is on the official page because tour groups treated tastings as a buffet. If you sample, buy — or decline. More on how vendors work: what to eat.

“If there's a chair on the street, you're meant to sit there”

The council has publicly disowned that seating. In identical year-end notices two years running (2024 and 2025 — the one part of the December package that is genuinely annual) it warns that some shops set chairs and tables out on the roadway, that repeated requests to stop have failed, and that customers are strongly advised to use shops with proper seating on their own premises. Roadway chairs are the unsafe option, not the sanctioned one — see practical for where to sit instead.

Rounding out the short list: never touch or poke the goods (manner #6 — open trays at hand height are stock), never open with a lower price (manner #7 — and Toyosu's retail arcade goes further: everything is fixed list price), and if you are not buying at a shop, the official map asks you to step aside and give way to buyers rather than stand talking mid-lane. None of this needs enforcement to matter: the working sanction at Tsukiji is simply that a shopkeeper decides you are not a customer.

One morning, four rule regimes

“Tsukiji etiquette” is not one list. A visitor who does Toyosu at dawn and Tsukiji for late morning crosses four separately published codes — and no English signage says so. The differences that actually catch people:

Outer-market lanes

The eight manners. Eating in front of the shop is fine; pros have priority to 09:00; ask-first photography (expect no). Run by the shopping-street association + council.

eat: shopfront OK · pros til 09:00 · calendar

Tsukiji Uogashi building

Same district, stricter code: no eating on the ground floor at all (even food bought outside), and the English guidance calls pre-09:00 shopping permission-only. Eat on the rooftop plaza or 3F instead.

eat: 1F banned · pre-09:00 restricted · the building

Toyosu Market (TMG)

Metropolitan-government regime: fixed visitor routes through three deck entrances, no eating or drinking in any corridor including on benches, no visitor smoking area, and you cannot buy from the wholesale floors.

eat: restaurants only · fixed route · Toyosu guide

Uogashi Yokocho arcade

Toyosu's 4F retail strip has its own manner guide: no eating anywhere inside, everything at fixed list price, no pets, no lockers or buggy parking — and turret trucks run through the sales aisles. Cash-centred.

eat: banned inside · fixed prices · at Toyosu

+ Senkyaku Banrai (private)

The adjoining Edo-style complex is private property: ordinary mall manners, no district code — and the only tattoo rule anywhere in this guide lives at its Manyo Club onsen. Neither market has any tattoo policy.

Groups: what “small numbers” means in practice

No maximum group size exists anywhere official — any guide quoting one invented it. The published standard is qualitative: small numbers. But the council has described its failure mode precisely — a coach-load arriving at once in lanes that are narrow, have no one-way system, and simply seize up. So the honest arithmetic: a family of 3–4 in single file with children's hands held is entirely within the norms; a party of 8+ moving as a block is exactly what manner #4 asks you not to do, in any month. In December 2025 one 30-person tour group handled it the right way: it split into small subgroups rather than cancel.

For genuinely large groups the district has its own answer, and it is almost unknown in English: the council's bookable classroom programme, Tsukiji Maruwakari Kouza 築地まるわかり講座 — ¥500 per head for parties of 20–50 (flat ¥10,000 below 20), 30–60 minutes inside the 09:00–11:00 window, Monday–Saturday except market closed days, at the council's own studio at Tsukiji 4-16-2. Content: how a market day works, 350 years of history, the shops, and how to shop well. Its leaflet prints the disclaimer in so many words: 「※注 場外ツアーではございません」 — this is NOT an outer-market tour. Booking is by fax from two months ahead, in Japanese with English by consultation. It is the council converting group demand into something the lanes can absorb — a classroom first, then small-number shopping.

The council trains guides rather than excluding them. It has run a paid guide seminar for working tour guides (¥1,000, 20 places, certificate of completion issued — the 2023 edition; no 2026 edition listed as of 31 October 2026), publishes the standing three-point guide protocol in English, and asks first-time guides to consult Plat Tsukiji before their first tour. A body trying to ban tourism does not issue guiding certificates. Guides who have done that homework are the ones worth booking — see where we stand.

How we square this with selling tours

This site earns a commission when you book a tour — so here is our position, stated rather than buried. The council's problem, in its own words, has never been guides; it is volume arriving as a block, in one month, in lanes built for wholesale. That maps cleanly onto what we do and don't feature:

  • We feature small-group formats. The walk we recommend first, Tsukiji Fish Market Guided Walking Tour (★ 4.8 · 2,006 reviews · ~$13, guide fee only — you buy your own food), is capped at 10 people — the shape the district's own protocol accommodates: small numbers, a guide who handles the ask-first conversations at shops, no block in the lane. Best for first-timers who want the etiquette handled for them at the price of a bowl of ramen.
  • Indoor formats don't crowd the lanes at all. Sushi classes happen in kitchens, and Toyosu-side auction tours run in a building with fixed visitor routes designed for exactly this. Both are unaffected by anything in the December story.
  • December, honestly: the 2025 request expired and had not been reissued for 2026 as of 31 October 2026 — but if you are booking a lane-based food tour or market walk for 25–30 December, re-check the council's news page (tsukiji.or.jp/info) from about 20 November, and ask your operator what they do in the year-end week. In 2025, at least one dining shop refused guide bookings for that week from the outset. Our default December advice mirrors the chairman's own: shop before noon, tour after — or take the class instead.
  • And the free option is official: the eight manners plus the council's free English map are the budget tour. DIY after 09:00, small and purposeful, breaks no rules and needs no booking. Every paid option above has to beat that honestly.
