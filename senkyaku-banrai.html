Toyosu · Senkyaku Banrai 千客万来 · verified 2 August 2026

“The ¥8,000 kaisendon tourist trap”? The honest verdict on Toyosu's Edo food hall — and its 24-hour onsen.

Senkyaku Banrai opened next to Toyosu Market in February 2024 and immediately became Japan's emblem of tourist pricing. Two years on, the honest verdict is neither the outrage nor the brochure: a polished, well-run tourist food hall where affordable food genuinely exists, with the one calendar in this whole story that never closes — and a hot-spring tower upstairs that quietly solves the auction visitor's 4 a.m. problem.

TL;DR

  • It's a tourist food hall, and a good one — judge it as that. Not a working market: the gritty everyday feel is next door at Toyosu Market and over at Tsukiji's lanes. What this building has instead: indoor climate control, seating, lifts, evening hours to 22:00, a free bay-view foot bath — and zero closing days, which makes it the answer to “it's a Wednesday / it's raining / it's 2 January”.
  • The ¥8,000 kaisendon is a viral-video artefact. The attributable 2024 figures were ¥6,980 (toro bowl) and ¥18,000 (the “Kōtei” uni bowl) — and the same building sells a ¥100 fish-cake cracker, ¥300 tuna skewers and sub-¥1,000 noodles. Both ends of the ladder are real.
  • The onsen is the underrated half. Manyo Club: ¥3,850 day pass (+¥150 bathing tax), open around the clock, and a ¥2,200 morning bath from 05:00 that dovetails exactly with an auction morning. Tattoos have a published workaround, not a flat ban.
  • One 2026 correction almost nothing in English carries: the free Shimbashi shuttle ended 31 May 2026 — the operator's own FAQ still advertises it. Take the Yurikamome or the Tokyo BRT instead; details in chapter 05.

What it is, and the reputation arc — honestly

Toyosu Senkyaku Banrai 豊洲 千客万来 is a private complex owned by hot-spring operator Manyo Club, on Tokyo Metropolitan land at 6-5-1 Toyosu — next to, but emphatically not part of, Toyosu Market at 6-6-1. Two connected buildings: a 3-storey Edo-styled food building (its market zone officially renamed Toyosu Jogai Shijo — “Toyosu Outer Market” — on 1 September 2025; the operator's own English pages still carry the old “Edomae Ichiba” name), and a 9-storey bathing tower housing the Tokyo Toyosu Manyo Club onsen. It was always planned as Toyosu's visitor-facing answer to Tsukiji's outer market; the relocation controversy delayed it from 2018 to a 1 February 2024 opening.

Then the arc, which is worth publishing whole rather than at either endpoint:

  • 2024 — the backlash. Within weeks of opening, its high-end seafood bowls became the national emblem of tourist pricing. The coined word インバウン丼 (“inbound-don” — a tourist-priced kaisendon) was nominated for the 2024 New Words and Buzzwords Award. The attributable figures behind the noise: a ¥6,980 toro-heavy bowl and an ¥18,000 uni bowl branded “Kōtei” (Emperor), with several ¥5,000+ bowls between them. The ¥6,980 shop's manager told media he uses never-frozen farmed bluefin from Uwajima and considers the price “completely appropriate” — and declined to treat the criticism as defamation.
  • 2025 — the quiet reset. The zone renamed itself on 1 September, tenants churned substantially (several counters named in viral 2024 write-ups are no longer on the floor guide), and the operator leaned into local demand: resident discounts, Saturday morning markets, beer gardens, bon-odori.
  • 2026 — the settled verdict. Among people who actually go: a polished, well-run tourist food hall where affordable options genuinely exist, missing the everyday-local feel of a working market. The 2024 outrage and the 2026 shrug are both true; this page holds both.
“The ¥8,000 kaisendon at Senkyaku Banrai”

A viral-video artefact, not a menu price. The documented 2024 figures are ¥6,980 (toro bowl) and ¥18,000 (“Kōtei” uni). The same building sells ¥100 crackers, ¥300 skewers and sub-¥1,000 noodles. Quote either end honestly — not the number from a stunt video.

“They charge foreigners more here”

No dual pricing operates. The name got attached to that debate only via the インバウン丼 buzzword. What the onsen actually runs is the opposite: small ID-verified discounts for people who live or work in Tokyo or Koto Ward — residency-based, not nationality-based.

What the cheap end actually looks like — a snapshot from named walk-throughs, so re-check at the counter: kamaboko-senbei fish-cake cracker ¥100 (Odawara Rokuzaemon, the best value in the building) · tamagoyaki on a stick from ¥300 (Marutake PREMIUM, the Tsukiji name) · charcoal-grilled tuna skewers ¥300–¥1,000 by cut (Sōma Suisan) · two Fukagawa clam croquettes ¥500 · fruit sandwich ¥500 · food-court ramen from about ¥950 and udon under ¥1,000. Grazing the 2F street properly costs about ¥1,500–2,500 a head. Tenant churn is real here — the official floor guide listed 63 tenants as of August 2026 against the “about 70” most press repeats — so check the live shop list on the operator's site before planning a visit around any single counter.

What you cannot do here: see the tuna auction or buy at wholesale — both are next door on a different calendar. And if the point of your trip is knives, the Tsukiji specialists all stayed at Tsukiji in 2018; the knife shop here is a convenience, not a destination — see knives & shopping. For the tuna-cutting show at Sushi Yasuke (Saturdays 11:30), see experiences & classes.

No closing days — the closed-day and rainy-day answer

The single most useful scheduling fact in the whole Tsukiji–Toyosu area: Senkyaku Banrai's official FAQ states, unambiguously, 「休業日はありません。豊洲市場の休市日にも営業しております。」 — “There are no closing days. We are open even on Toyosu Market's closed days.” The only caveat is ad-hoc closure for equipment inspection or severe weather, posted on its homepage. Four calendars operate within 3 km, and this is the only one with nothing on it:

Sun/hol closed · pros before 09:00

The practical translation: on a designated Wednesday, a Sunday, a public holiday, or 1–4 January — when the wholesale market and most of the Tsukiji story are dark — this complex is open and busy. It is also fully indoor and climate-controlled, which makes it the default wet-weather and heatwave plan; the Tsukiji lanes are outdoor and unsheltered. One orientation trap: the pedestrian deck from Shijō-mae serves both Toyosu Market and Senkyaku Banrai, and they read as one building from the station. On a market closed day, walk past the shuttered market walkways — Senkyaku Banrai is trading 100 m on.

The Manyo Club onsen: full price table, real rules

The bathing tower — Tokyo Toyosu Manyo Club — is open 24 hours, 365 days, though the large baths themselves close 03:00–05:00 (changing rooms 03:30–05:00) for cleaning. The water is genuine hot-spring water trucked in daily by tank lorry from Hakone and Yugawara — a modern echo of Edo-period spa-water deliveries to the shoguns. Reception is on 7F; day bathing needs no reservation, ever.

The tattoo rule, verbatim

Tattoos are refused in principle — with a precisely published exception. A tattoo coverable by up to two designated cover seals, each max 11 × 20 cm (¥110 each at reception), is admitted with the seals applied — and they must stay on for your entire stay in the building, not just in the bath. A larger tattoo, or refusal to use the seals, means refused entry; entering without declaring one means immediate removal. Tattoo stickers and body paint fall under the same rule.

Free foot bath vs paid foot bath

Two foot baths, endlessly confused. The 8F Senkyaku Banrai Foot Bath Garden is free to anyone, 09:00–22:00, no onsen admission needed, with the Tokyo Bay and Rainbow Bridge view — it fills around 18:00–19:00 for the night view, and nothing is provided, so bring a towel (a vending machine reportedly sells them for about ¥200 — a third-party figure we couldn't confirm officially). The 10F rooftop Observation Foot Bath Garden is for Manyo Club admission-holders only. Family small print worth knowing before you pay: children still in nappies may not enter the shared tubs (baby baths exist, and the private family baths admit them); from age 7 — or 120 cm — children must use the bath of their own gender; under-18s must be accompanied by a guardian; the stone spa is adults-only.

The sequence almost no English guide prints

The morning bath runs 05:00–09:00 at ¥2,200 — the cheapest and quietest window — and it lines up exactly with an auction morning next door: bath at 05:00, market by 06:30, sushi breakfast, back here for the 10:00 opening. Or invert it: do the auction first and soak afterwards. If you'd rather have the dawn choreographed for you, this Toyosu auction-and-breakfast morning (★ 4.6 · 164 reviews · ~$114, 31 October 2026) handles the walkway viewing and the Sushi Dai/Bentomi breakfast queue — then you're 100 m from the onsen with the whole complex about to open. It's the right shape for a first-timer pairing auction, breakfast and bath in one arc; more options on the auction tours page. Staying the night before works too: recliner-and-lounge access past 03:00 costs the ¥3,000 late-night surcharge, while an actual bedroom is the separate hotel product from ¥14,800 — don't confuse the two.

Getting there — and the shuttle that no longer exists

“Take the free shuttle bus from Shimbashi”

Ended 31 May 2026. The Shimbashi ↔ Senkyaku Banrai free shuttle was discontinued — and as of 2 August 2026 the operator's own FAQ page still advertised it, contradicting its own news post. Only the Monzen-nakachō shuttle survives, on a revised timetable: 25 visitor seats, staff board first, facility users only. From Shimbashi the operator now points you to the Yurikamome or the Tokyo BRT (~10 min).

  • Rail: Yurikamome to Shijō-mae 市場前, then 4 minutes on the level-2 pedestrian deck straight into the building — step-free the whole way, no street level. Note Shijō-mae is an unstaffed station: a ticket problem at 05:30 has no window to solve it.
  • Tokyo BRT: 3 minutes' walk from Toyosu-shijō-mae — the cheaper, faster route from Shimbashi now the shuttle is gone.
  • Airport buses: highway buses link Michi-no-Terasu Toyosu (6 min walk) with Haneda in ~35 minutes (3 round trips/day) and Narita in ~70 (1 round trip) — a genuinely underused arrival: land early, bathe, eat, then check in to your hotel.
  • Car: unusually for this story, there is parking — ¥300 per 20 min for facility users, validated free for 2/3/5 hours at ¥2,000/¥3,000/¥5,000 spend (2F Banya counter, receipts + ticket); Manyo Club users ¥1,200 up to 7 hours. Toyosu Market next door has none at all. The facility itself warns of congestion and asks you to come by train.
  • Boat: a landing reached by the 2F connecting bridge (opened December 2024) runs a yakatabune sightseeing cruise under the Rainbow Bridge; operating days vary monthly and tickets are sold on site at the 2F Edomae Kisen office — ask there rather than planning around it.

Practical: crowds, kids, and the small print

  • When to come: weekday 10:00–12:00; on weekends, arrive before the 10:00 opening. Weekend and holiday 11:00–15:00 is the peak — visitor reports from mid-2026 (unverified, but consistent) put waits at 30–60 minutes at the famous kaisendon counters, and the binding constraint is usually a seat, not the food queue: the 2F street is stand-and-eat, so sit-down eaters should head straight to 3F (food court, kids' area) or 1F. Koto City's official figures put the food building at roughly 400,000 purchases a month through 2024 — above forecast.
  • Eating while walking is fine here — the 2F alley is designed for it. That's the opposite of the Tsukiji lanes' manners; don't carry the assumption in either direction. See visiting responsibly.
  • No stroller storage — flatly. The FAQ says strollers cannot be checked anywhere in the complex (2F coin lockers take bags only). Tsukiji's Plat information centre, by contrast, stores one for ¥500. Otherwise this is the most family-tolerant stop in the whole story: step-free from the station, lifts, nursing rooms (2F bathing building and 3F), accessible toilets on three floors, a 10:00 start instead of a 4:30 alarm. Wheelchairs are loaned but stock is limited — request ahead.
  • A prayer room 礼拝室 sits on 2F of the bathing building — rare in Tokyo commercial facilities and worth knowing for Muslim travellers.
  • Payment: far more card-friendly than either market, but shop-by-shop. Cards, QR and transport IC work at the onsen, the sit-down restaurants and the 3F food court; carry ¥5,000–10,000 cash for the small 2F stalls. Full picture on practical & accessibility.
  • Free things beyond walking around: the 8F foot bath (09:00–22:00); the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's “Ichiba no Hiroba” PR corner on 2F, with wholesale-market exhibits and monthly hands-on programmes; and — in principle on the third Saturday of each month — TMG's free Toyosu Market introduction tour for Senkyaku Banrai visitors. That last one runs intermittently: confirm the current schedule at the 2F counter before building a day around it. If you want a guided market walk you can actually book, see market walks.
  • Wi-Fi is free throughout (SSID SENKYAKU_BANRAI); the Banya information counter on 2F (09:00–22:00) handles lost property and parking validation.

The clock, if you're pairing it with the market

  1. 05:00Manyo Club morning bath opens (¥2,200) — the quiet window used by locals and market-bound early risers.
  2. 06:30Next door: Toyosu Market walkways and restaurant queues — on trading days only.
  3. 09:00Free 8F foot bath and the Banya counter open.
  4. 10:00The complex opens — the 2F Edo street's best hour. Onsen day-pass reception opens on 7F.
  5. 15:00Weekend crowds thin from here.
  6. 18:002F retail alley closes; the 8F foot bath fills for the night view — shift ten minutes either side.
  7. 22:00Restaurants and foot bath close. The onsen, one bar (to 05:00) and the recliner floors carry the night.
  8. 03:00Large baths shut for cleaning until 05:00 — arrive at 03:30 and you'll wait in a recliner.

How long to allow: ninety minutes covers all three food floors, one thing eaten and the free foot bath — a legitimate visit. Two to three hours to graze properly and sit down on 3F; add four to five if you use the onsen well, which turns it into a half day. The full Toyosu day — auction at 05:15, market, then this from 10:00 — runs 6–8 hours and only works on a market trading day.

The myth wall

“Senkyaku Banrai is part of Toyosu Market”

Separate everything. Different address (6-5-1 vs 6-6-1), owner (Manyo Club vs the Tokyo Metropolitan Government), hours, calendar and rules. The market closes 114 days a year; this closes zero. On a closed Wednesday one is shuttered and the other is trading, 100 m apart.

“You can watch the tuna auction from Senkyaku Banrai”

No. The auction is inside Toyosu Market: free from the market's own 2F windows, or the deck by monthly lottery — mechanics on the auction page. Nothing here includes it, and no auction tour can sell it.

“Sushi Dai and Daiwa are in Senkyaku Banrai”

Both are inside Toyosu Market, where they moved from Tsukiji in 2018, with storefront same-day slot systems from ~05:00. Senkyaku Banrai has its own, different sushi counters. The full name-collision story is on sushi breakfast.

“Manyo Club pulled out — the project was cancelled”

2017-era news, never updated. The operator signalled withdrawal in 2017 during the Tsukiji-redevelopment dispute, stayed, built, and opened on 1 February 2024. It has been trading ever since — the history is on history & redevelopment.

“The onsen is a natural Toyosu hot spring”

Trucked in daily by tank lorry from the sources at Hakone and Yugawara — a modern echo of Edo-period spa-water deliveries to the shogunate. Real spring water; not local.

“Stay the night for the ¥3,000 surcharge”

That buys a recliner, not a room. The ¥3,000 late-night surcharge covers staying in the lounges past 03:00 until 09:00. A bedroom is the separate hotel product from ¥14,800 per adult with breakfast, two sharing.
